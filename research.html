Research plan

Research question and hypothesis

Research question

How does changing a low-cost photocatalyst or reactor configuration affect the rate of hydrogen and oxygen evolution under solar illumination, and which design gives the most credible evidence for improvement?

Hypothesis

A specific low-cost photocatalyst or reactor design with a more favorable surface area, light capture, and stable particle dispersion will increase measured gas-production rates more than a simple baseline control, but only if both gases are verified quantitatively and the design is repeatable.

Objectives

  1. Review published work on solar photocatalytic water splitting and identify a cost-sensitive research gap.
  2. Design a simple, supervised experiment using controlled variables and clear trial labels.
  3. Measure and record light intensity, temperature, exposure time, and any verified gas output.
  4. Analyze patterns in the data while distinguishing correlation from proof of cause.
  5. Present the findings with clear limits and a transparent discussion of uncertainty.

Variables

Independent variable

Photocatalyst material, particle size, coating, or reactor geometry.

Dependent variable

Measured rate of hydrogen and/or oxygen evolution, if verified and reported in comparable units.

Controlled variables

Light intensity, reactor volume, temperature range, exposure duration, sample mass, and measurement method.

Control groups and comparison logic

A control group should use the simplest baseline design with the same reactor conditions but without the specific material or configuration being tested. This helps you compare whether the change is associated with improvement.

  • Baseline reactor without added photocatalyst enhancement.
  • Same light source and temperature range as the test design.
  • Same duration and sample volume to reduce confounding.
  • Only compare gas-production rates when the units are compatible and the measurement is independently verified.

Research gap

Photocatalytic water splitting is established research. My project should not claim a new discovery before the literature supports a specific gap. A realistic science-fair aim is to compare low-cost materials, reactor configuration, or operating conditions and identify a testable improvement that is justified by published work, careful design, and honest data.
